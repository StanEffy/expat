# Psychometric Instruments and Gamified "D&D Alignment/Class" Framing for a Voluntary Co-founder Matching Test

Scope note: research done 2026-10-20 with a limited budget of 15 tool calls. Licensing was checked on primary pages where they could be reached: ipip.ori.org, hexaco.org, and the BFI-2 lab pages, which were reached through search snippets because colby.edu returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch. Anything labelled "unverified" comes from background knowledge and has not been confirmed by a source in this session. The report writer should treat those items as gaps.

## 1. Big Five / HEXACO short forms: items, licence (incl. commercial), reliability, languages

### Takeaway
IPIP-based instruments are the only Big Five/HEXACO-family option with an explicit, primary-source grant for **commercial** use. The IPIP ones are Mini-IPIP (20), IPIP-NEO-120, IPIP-HEXACO and Mini-IPIP6. HEXACO-PI-R (60/100) is free only for non-profit academic research, and commercial use requires contacting the authors. The BFI-2 family (60 / BFI-2-S 30 / BFI-2-XS 15) is copyrighted, and the Berkeley lab states it is "for non-commercial uses only." TIPI (10 items) has very low internal consistency. **Recommendation: build on IPIP items (public domain).**

### Cited Findings
**IPIP (ipip.ori.org)**
- IPIP items are public domain. Permission "has already been automatically granted for any person to use IPIP items… for any purpose, commercial or non-commercial", and no contact with the site author is needed. This covers the Mini-IPIP, the IPIP-NEO-120 and the IPIP-HEXACO scales, which are all built from IPIP items. — [IPIP Permission page](https://ipip.ori.org/newPermission.htm)
- The Mini-IPIP6 adds an Honesty-Humility scale and so gives a 6-factor HEXACO-like structure from IPIP items. In a Serbian sample of 218 undergraduates, all alphas were above .70. — [Primenjena psihologija, Mini IPIP-6](https://primenjena.psihologija.ff.uns.ac.rs/index.php/pp/article/view/2042)
- A Serbian comparison of short HEXACO measures (Dinić 2018, N=310) found satisfactory CFA fit for the Mini-IPIP6, marginal fit for the 24QB6, and unsatisfactory fit for the BHI. — [Primenjena psihologija 2018](https://primenjena.psihologija.ff.uns.ac.rs/index.php/pp/article/view/2113)

**HEXACO-PI-R (hexaco.org)**
- The 100-item and 60-item forms may be downloaded free "only for the purpose of non-profit academic research." Online versions must be password-protected or hidden from search engines. Non-academic uses, **including commercial use**, require contacting the authors (Lee & Ashton). — [hexaco.org inventory page](https://hexaco.org/hexaco-inventory)
- Translations are listed for Russian (self-report, 60 and 100), Ukrainian (self-report, 60 and 100), Spanish (two translations: Belloch with self- and observer forms, Ortiz & Romero with self-report) and Arabic (an Excel file with six versions). — [hexaco.org inventory page](https://hexaco.org/hexaco-inventory)

**Brief HEXACO Inventory (BHI, 24 items)**
- De Vries (2013), *Journal of Research in Personality* 47, 871–880. There is one item per HEXACO facet (24 facets, 4 per factor). — [Primenjena psihologija 2018](https://primenjena.psihologija.ff.uns.ac.rs/index.php/pp/article/view/2113); [hexaco.org references](https://hexaco.org/references)
- Internal consistency is low, with alphas of roughly .43–.72 in a Serbian validation. Test-retest stability and convergence with HEXACO-100 were relatively good, but CFA fit was poor. — [Primenjena psihologija 2018](https://primenjena.psihologija.ff.uns.ac.rs/index.php/pp/article/view/2113)
- A Persian validation in university students reported acceptable reliability and validity. — [JEM, Persian BHI](https://jem.atu.ac.ir/article_10526_en.html)
- BHI licensing was not verified (see Gaps).

**BFI-2 / BFI-2-S (30) / BFI-2-XS (15)**
- Items are copyright 2015 by Oliver P. John and Christopher J. Soto. The Colby page grants "permission for personal and research use" and does not mention commercial use. — [Colby Personality Lab BFI-2](https://www.colby.edu/academics/departments-and-programs/psychology/research-opportunities/personality-lab/the-bfi-2/) (seen via search snippet; direct fetch returned 403)
- The Berkeley John Lab says "At this time, the BFI-2 is for non-commercial uses only" and that commercial requests should go to ucbpersonalitylab@gmail.com. — [Berkeley John Lab BFI-2](https://ocf.berkeley.edu/~johnlab/bfi.html)
- Translations exist. A Spanish BFI-2-XS form is hosted by Colby. — [Colby Spanish BFI-2-XS PDF](https://www.colby.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/bfi2xs-form-spanish.pdf)
- Russian BFI-2: Kalugin, Shchebetenko, Mishkevich, Soto & John (2021), HSE Psychology Journal, online N=1,787. The five-factor structure held at domain and facet level, with strict measurement invariance across sex. — [HSE Psychology Journal 2021](https://psy-journal.hse.ru/en/2021-18-1/456529358.html)

**TIPI (10 items, Gosling et al. 2003)**
- Original alphas were low: E .68, A .40, C .50, N .73, O .45. Six-week test-retest was r ≈ .62–.77. — [Testable TIPI](https://www.testable.org/scale/tipi-ten-item-personality-inventory-big-five); [Frontiers 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00461/full)
- Gosling's own note says the TIPI was designed to maximise validity, not alpha. — [Gosling lab note on TIPI alpha](https://gosling.psy.utexas.edu/scales-weve-developed/ten-item-personality-measure-tipi/a-note-on-alpha-reliability-and-factor-structure-in-the-tipi/)
- A Russian TIPI-RU was validated by Sergeeva, Kirillov & Dzhumagulova (2016), N=218 students plus a retest subsample of 51. Consistency was acceptable except Openness, and convergence with the 5PFQ was good. — [Experimental Psychology (Russia) 2016](https://psyjournals.ru/en/journals/exppsy/archive/2016_n3/sergeeva)

### Inferences
- For a commercial app, the safe default is **Mini-IPIP6 (24 items, 6 factors incl. Honesty-Humility)**, or Mini-IPIP (20) plus IPIP H-H items. The IPIP-HEXACO and IPIP-NEO-120 can serve as an optional "deep dive" mode. All are public domain.
- HEXACO-60 would be scientifically preferable for Honesty-Humility, which is highly relevant to trust between co-founders. The app would need written permission from Lee & Ashton, and online deployment conflicts with the "password-protected / non-searchable" condition unless the app is behind a login.
- BFI-2-S/XS should be avoided unless a commercial licence is negotiated.
- TIPI and BHI are too unreliable at the scale level to support person-to-person matching. Matching amplifies measurement error because difference scores are less reliable than either score.

### Gaps
- **Mini-IPIP reliability (Donnellan et al., 2006)** was not retrieved. From background knowledge (unverified), alphas are about .60–.80.
- **IPIP-NEO-120 reliability (Johnson 2014)** was not retrieved.
- Validated **Russian/Ukrainian/Arabic translations of the Mini-IPIP or IPIP-HEXACO** were not found in this session. ipip.ori.org hosts a translations list that should be checked; it was not fetched.
- **BHI licence:** unclear whether it falls under hexaco.org terms.
- **TIPI licence:** not verified. It is widely reported as free for research.

## 2. Supplementary short scales for entrepreneurship and cooperation

### Takeaway
Two add-ons are free, short and well validated. The **single-item general risk question** (0–10, Dohmen et al. 2011, used in SOEP) is validated against incentivised lotteries. The **SVO Slider Measure** (Murphy et al. 2011) is free and has high test-retest reliability. The **PVQ-21 / ESS Human Values Scale** is usable, though cross-cultural invariance is weaker than for the PVQ-40. Validated free conflict-style instruments are murkier. Licensing for ROCI-II and DUTCH was not confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- **General risk question:** "how willing are you to take risks, in general?", rated from 0 (unwilling) to 10 (fully prepared). Dohmen et al. (2011, JEEA) validated it against paid lottery choices in a representative field experiment of about 450 people. It predicted a broad set of risky behaviours, while the lottery measure did not. — [DIW SOEPpaper](https://www.diw.de/documents/publikationen/73/diw_01.c.438759.de/diw_sp0630.pdf); [Maastricht CRIS record](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/individual-risk-attitudes-measurement-determinants-and-behavioral-3/)
- A two-wave study (N=434) found good reliability for the risk item and convergence with multi-item measures, but lower predictive validity than multi-item measures for most outcomes. — [NSF PAR paper](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10608738)
- Swiss conference abstracts (IMEBESS 2026) report that the single item predicted incentivised lottery behaviour better than a 10-item scale (students, n=486). These have not been peer-reviewed. — [IMEBESS 2026 abstract](https://coms.events/imebess26/data/abstracts/en/abstract_0093_0198_0093.html)
- **SVO Slider:** Murphy, Ackermann & Handgraaf (2011), *Judgment and Decision Making* 6(8), 771–781. The authors invite others to use it freely. It has 6 primary items and gives a continuous SVO angle (prosocial / individualistic / competitive / altruistic). — [SJDM SVO Slider page](https://dlab.sauder.ubc.ca/sjdm/dmidi/SVO%20Slider.html); [Murphy et al. 2011 PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/78981D731BFB89AFCFC789D40FD8C11F/S1930297500004204a.pdf/measuring-social-value-orientation.pdf)
- SVO test-retest: ICC = .78 over 6 monthly waves (N=495; de Matos Fernandes, Bakker & Dijkstra 2022). A short-term r ≈ .915 is reported on the SJDM summary but was not verified against the original. — [JDM 2022](https://dlab.sauder.ubc.ca/sjdm/journal/21/211119/jdm211119.html); [SJDM page](https://dlab.sauder.ubc.ca/sjdm/dmidi/SVO%20Slider.html)
- **PVQ-21 / ESS Human Values Scale:** the ESS21 is listed in a repository of Schwartz value scales "with numerous authorized language versions." — [HUJI repository record](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/a-repository-of-schwartz-value-scales-with-instructions-and-an-in/)
- PVQ-21 invariance: German–Polish comparison (N=1,204). The PVQ-21 reached partial scalar invariance for only 7 values, against 9 of 10 for the PVQ-40. — [SSOAR, Cieciuch & Davidov](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/handle/document/44223)
- A German ESS 2003 validation confirmed the structure, but four value types had to be merged. — [HUJI record, Schmidt et al. 2007](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/die-messung-von-werten-mit-dem-portraits-value-questionnaire/)
- **ROCI-II (Rahim):** 28 items on a 5-point scale, with Forms A, B and C (supervisor, subordinates, peers). The only CC licence found (CC BY 3.0) covers an ENACT learning document, not necessarily the instrument. — [ENACT ROCI-II PDF](https://mondodigitale.org/sites/default/files/ROCI-II.pdf)
- **DUTCH:** revised versions exist and have been validated in Chinese students, where problem-solving and compromising merged into one factor. Free-use status was not confirmed. — [Hogrefe EJPA](https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1027/1015-5759/a000527); [SBP journal](https://sbp-journal.com/index.php/sbp/article/view/7768)

### Inferences
- A compact "co-founder module" could be: 1 risk item, 6 SVO slider items, and the 21 PVQ items (optional). That adds about 28 items on top of 24 Mini-IPIP6 items, roughly 6–8 minutes in total.
- PVQ-21 values are best shown descriptively (e.g., "what drives you") and not used for hard matching, given the weak cross-cultural invariance among a multinational user base.
- The ESS has fielded the PVQ-21 in many European languages, including Russian (ESS Russia rounds), Ukrainian and Spanish. These official translations are a practical source, but the specific language files were not verified this session.

### Gaps
- Not researched within budget: **DOSPERT** (Blais & Weber 2006, 30 items; licence and translations unverified), **Entrepreneurial self-efficacy** (e.g., McGee et al. 2009), **Individual EO scale** (Bolton & Lane 2012, 10 items), **Short Grit Scale** (Duckworth & Quinn 2009, Grit-S 8 items; free for non-commercial use per Duckworth lab, unverified), and **regulatory focus** (Higgins RFQ / Lockwood scale). Each needs a licence check.
- The De Dreu et al. (2001) DUTCH 20-item item list and permission statement were not retrieved.
- Validated Arabic or Ukrainian versions of the risk item and the SVO slider were not checked. The risk item is used in Falk et al.'s Global Preference Survey across 76 countries (background knowledge, unverified here), so translations likely exist.

## 3. Gamification and game-based assessment: effects on validity, response quality, completion, faking

### Takeaway
Light gamification of an existing questionnaire changes the experience more than the measurement. Examples are narrative wrappers, progress bars, badges and themed visuals. Evidence shows clear gains in enjoyment and engagement but inconsistent effects on completion and data quality. Converting items into a story or situational format can keep convergence with the original scales, but this must be re-validated. Adding graphics did not improve measurement. Faking resistance of game-based assessments is still unproven.

### Cited Findings
- Landers & Sanchez (2022) distinguish **game-based assessment**, where traits are inferred from gameplay, from **gamified assessment**, where game elements are added to an existing assessment. This matters because the latter keeps the original items' validity evidence more directly. — [Landers & Sanchez 2022 summary](https://universityxp.com/research/2022/9/17/game-based-gamified-and-gamefully-designed-assessments-for-employee-selection-definitions-distinctions-design-and-validation)
- Landers & Collmus (2022, Frontiers in Psychology) converted each item of an existing personality measure into a story-embedded situational item. They examined convergence, incremental prediction, faking and applicant reactions. The full results were not extracted because the EPUB fetch failed. — [Frontiers 2022 fpsyg.2022.812918](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.812918/epub)
- Armstrong, Landers & Collmus (2016) are cited for evidence that game-based measures can predict job performance beyond traditional methods. — [Nikolaou et al., Cambridge](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/39125B6F3BD27F6B4C1AD514D2330E4A/S1138741619000052a.pdf/exploring-the-relationship-of-a-gamified-assessment-with-performance.pdf)
- Harman & Brown (2020, SJDM poster): a text-based game-based personality measure adequately measured personality in a shorter, more engaging task, and **adding graphics did not improve measurement**. — [SJDM 2020 poster](https://dlab.sauder.ubc.ca/sjdm/presentations/2020-Poster-Harman-Jason_L.-gamification-personality-assessment.pdf)
- Ramos-Villagrasa & Fernández-del-Río (2023, JWOP) say research on game-related assessments warrants skepticism. Claims of reduced faking with retained validity are still being tested. — [JWOP 2023](https://journals.copmadrid.org/jwop/art/jwop2023a18)
- Keusch & Zhang's systematic review of gamified surveys: game elements generally improve enjoyment and interest, but effects on completion, break-off and data quality are unclear. — [Mannheim MADOC](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/41417/)
- Mavletova (2014; 1,050 Russian children and adolescents) found no difference in drop-out between text, visual and gamified versions on desktop. On mobile, the gamified version had the fewest drop-outs. This comes from a secondary summary. — [NeoAcademic (Landers blog)](https://neoacademic.com/2014/10/02/gamifying-surveys-increase-completion-rate-data-quality/)
- A SIOP call on "Game-based and Gamified Assessments: Advances at the Frontier of Psychometrics" shows this is an active, unsettled research area. — [SIOP](https://www.siop.org/Career-Center/Calls-and-Announcements/ArtMID/19859/ArticleID/3485/Game-based-and-Gamified-Assessments-Advances-at-the-Frontier-of-Psychometrics)

### Inferences (design guidance)
- **Gamify the wrapper, not the items.** Keep the validated item wording and response scale verbatim, or use validated translations. Add theme through the intro narrative, a "character sheet" progress UI, chapter breaks (one per domain) and the reveal at the end. This is the lowest-risk way to keep the norms and reliability evidence.
- If items are rewritten into fantasy scenarios (e.g., "The dragon blocks the road: do you…"), treat the result as a **new instrument**. Pilot it alongside the original items and check convergence (r ≥ .6–.7 with the source scale), as Landers & Collmus did.
- Graphics are fine for UX but should not be expected to improve measurement (Harman & Brown).
- **Faking risk is real here because results are shown to potential partners**, which is a self-presentation context similar to applicant settings. Mitigations, which are inferences and not tested in this session:
  1. Show partners only the high-level archetype and compatibility notes, not raw scores.
  2. Allow private-only mode.
  3. Frame every pole as an asset, so there is no "evil" or "bad" end.
  4. Add a few attention-check or infrequency items.
  5. Avoid revealing the scoring key mid-test.
- Store **raw item responses and continuous domain scores** in the database. Derive archetypes at display time so the mapping can be revised without retesting.

### Gaps
- Effect sizes from Landers & Collmus (2022) on convergence and faking were not extracted, because the EPUB was unreadable. The full HTML version should be fetched.
- No study was found on how **sharing results with potential partners** (as opposed to employers) affects social desirability in personality self-reports.
- The "Bailey, Pritchard & Kernohan" gamification meta-analysis could not be located.

## 4. Mapping personality to D&D alignment/classes; MBTI/DISC/16Personalities critique

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed study was found that maps D&D alignment to Big Five or HEXACO traits. Any mapping is a design choice that the app must own and disclose. The type-vs-dimension problem that undermines MBTI applies equally to a 9-box alignment grid. The safe pattern is: score continuous dimensions, show a fun archetype as a label on top, and always show the underlying dimension bars.

### Cited Findings
- The only D&D-personality study found was Lorenz, Hagitte & Brandt (2022, Frontiers in Psychology), N=801 (399 players, 402 non-players). Players scored higher on Extraversion and Openness, and players' Extraversion correlated with their characters' Charisma. It does not address alignment. — [PMC9627618](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9627618/)
- MBTI test-retest: Pittenger reported that roughly half of retakers get a different type after about five weeks. Secondary sources give 39–76% (citing Pittenger 2005). Capraro & Capraro's (2002) meta-analysis gives an overall reliability of about .815 (range .48–.97) for the continuous scales. — [Fortune 2013](https://fortune.com/2013/05/15/have-we-all-been-duped-by-the-myers-briggs-test); [Recruiter.com critique](https://www.recruiter.com/i/critique-of-the-myers-briggs-type-indicator-critique/); [Team Technology](https://teamtechnology.co.uk/myers-briggs-criticisms.html) (secondary, lower authority)
- Type dichotomy problem: people near the midpoint with almost identical scores receive opposite labels. MBTI score distributions are not bimodal, which undermines the "types" premise. — [Team Technology](https://teamtechnology.co.uk/myers-briggs-criticisms.html); [earlyyears.tv](https://www.earlyyears.tv/mbti-test-accuracy/) (secondary)
- A counterpoint: MBTI's continuous scores correlate with Big Five traits, for example about .74 with Extraversion. The scales therefore measure something, and the problem is the typology. — [Psychology Today, "In defense of the Myers-Briggs"](https://www.psychologytoday.com/za/blog/my-brothers-keeper/202002/in-defense-the-myers-briggs)

### Inferences (mapping design)
- **Suggested mapping, as a heuristic and not validated:**
  - Lawful↔Chaotic: Conscientiousness (orderliness and dutifulness facets), with Openness and low risk aversion pushing toward "Chaotic." The risk item and Openness could tilt this axis.
  - Good↔Evil: do **not** label anyone "Evil." Use a cooperation axis instead, built from Honesty-Humility, Agreeableness and SVO angle, with pole names like "Guardian" vs "Mercenary/Free Agent." Label both poles positively.
  - Classes: derive from a profile on the dominant two traits. For example:
    - high E + high A → Bard (connector, sales/BD)
    - high C + low risk → Paladin/Cleric (operations, finance)
    - high O + high risk → Wizard/Sorcerer (product, R&D)
    - high E + high risk + low A → Fighter/Barbarian (execution, hustle)
    - high C + high O → Artificer (builder)
    - low E + high O → Druid/Ranger (independent specialist)
- **Why the 9-box grid is dangerous:**
  - It dichotomises continuous traits, which is the MBTI flaw: people near the cut-offs flip on retest, and short scales like TIPI and BHI make this worse.
  - Barnum or Forer effects make any flattering archetype feel accurate. This is background knowledge, unverified here, but well established (Forer 1949).
  - "Evil" or "Chaotic" labels could stigmatise users or be misread by partners, especially across cultures.
- **Mitigations:**
  - Show percentile bars with uncertainty bands next to the archetype.
  - Mark "borderline" users as hybrid classes, e.g., "Paladin/Bard multiclass." Multiclassing is a natural D&D mechanic for near-midpoint scores.
  - Disclose that archetypes are a playful summary and not a validated typology.
  - Match on **raw dimension scores**, mainly complementarity on skills/roles and similarity on values, H-H and SVO, not on class labels.
  - State explicitly that the test is not for hiring.
- **Why not MBTI/16Personalities/DISC:**
  - MBTI is proprietary (The Myers-Briggs Company) and has dichotomy and retest problems.
  - 16Personalities is a proprietary NERIS model that borrows MBTI letter codes. Its licence would bar reuse. This is background knowledge, unverified.
  - DISC has many proprietary versions and little peer-reviewed validity evidence (unverified, see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No primary source was found for **DISC** validity or reliability. That search returned nothing.
- No empirical study was found mapping the **alignment grid to HEXACO or Big Five**. Any such mapping is the app's own construct and should be piloted. One option is to ask users to self-select an alignment and correlate that with their trait scores.
- Barnum/Forer effect citations and 16Personalities licence and model details were not retrieved this session.

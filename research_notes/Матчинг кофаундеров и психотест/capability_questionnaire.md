# Capabilities & Aspirations Questionnaire for Co-founder / Cooperation Matching (Expat App)

Research date: 2026-10-20. Scope: frameworks and best practices for a self-reported CAN / WANT / COULD + resources questionnaire for expats forming complementary venture teams (tech and non-tech). EU focus where jurisdiction matters. Note: sources were mostly reviewed via search snippets/abstracts; full texts were not read unless stated.

## 1. Effectuation (Sarasvathy): operationalizing bird-in-hand means and affordable loss

### Takeaway
Effectuation gives the questionnaire its spine. Bird-in-hand means are "who I am / what I know / whom I know", which maps to identity and interests, skills, and network. Affordable loss is a better way to ask about commitment than asking for expected returns: ask what money, time and emotional cost each person is willing to lose. Validated scales exist (Chandler et al. 2011), but they measure decision processes in existing ventures, so the app needs its own adapted questions.

### Cited Findings
- Bird-in-hand: expert entrepreneurs start from their means rather than a fixed goal. Means are identity (traits, tastes, abilities), knowledge, and networks. — [KU Innovation (Sarasvathy effectuation model)](https://innovationenglish.sites.ku.dk/model/sarasvathy-effectuation/); [NSRCEL Founder's Handbook to Effectuation](https://nsrcel.org/founders-guide-to-effectuation/)
- Affordable loss: decide by how much you can afford to lose (money and emotionally) instead of by expected return. Partners each commit only what they can afford to lose. — [NSRCEL](https://nsrcel.org/founders-guide-to-effectuation/); [Wade Institute tool: effectuation](https://studio.wadeinstitute.org.au/tool-detail-effectuation.html)
- The other principles are Crazy Quilt (partnerships and pre-commitments from self-selected stakeholders), Lemonade (use surprises), and Pilot-in-the-plane (control rather than prediction). Sources disagree on whether there are 4 or 5 principles and on the study details (1997 study of 27 experts versus the 2001 publication). — [KU Innovation](https://innovationenglish.sites.ku.dk/model/sarasvathy-effectuation/); [Military.com summary](https://mst.military.com/veteran-jobs/5-principles-of-effectuation-new-way-of-problem-solving-veteran-entrepreneurs.html)
- Measurement: Chandler, DeTienne, McKelvie & Mumford (2011, JBV 26(3)) validated causation as unidimensional and effectuation as formative and multidimensional. Its sub-dimensions are experimentation, affordable loss and flexibility, plus pre-commitments, which is shared with causation. Experimentation correlates positively with uncertainty. — [Effectuation.org: Causation and effectuation processes – a validation study](https://effectuation.org/publications-library/causation-and-effectuation-processes-a-validation-study)
- A 2019 follow-up reviewing 81 empirical studies found that measurement tensions limit the accumulation of knowledge on effectuation. — [Effectuation.org: The measurement of effectuation](https://effectuation.org/publications-library/the-measurement-of-effectuation-highlighting-research-tensions-and-opportunities-for-the-future)

### Inferences
- Map to question blocks:
  - **Who I am** covers identity, values, interests (RIASEC), work style, and what I would never do.
  - **What I know** covers skills (CAN), learnable skills (COULD), and domain/industry knowledge, including home-country market knowledge.
  - **Whom I know** covers network items such as "I can personally introduce you to: suppliers in X, customers in Y, landlords, officials, diaspora community groups", plus host- and home-country network size and type.
- **Affordable loss questions** (concrete, numeric, banded):
  - "Max money I'd put in during the first 12 months without regret: 0 / <€1k / €1–5k / €5–20k / €20k+"
  - "Hours per week I can sustain for 6 months"
  - "How long could I go without income from this venture?"
  - "What I am NOT willing to risk" (permit status, family savings, current job)
- **Crazy Quilt / pre-commitment questions:** "What could you commit TODAY to a partner?" Examples are a workspace, equipment, a first customer, a supplier contact, or a translation service. This turns network into concrete, matchable assets.
- **Lemonade/flexibility:** ask about openness to pivot. One item is "I'd join a venture in a sector I haven't chosen if the team is right" (yes / maybe / no). This helps match goal-driven (causal) and means-driven (effectual) people.
- Do not copy the Chandler scale items verbatim. They refer to an existing venture's past decisions. Adapt them as "intent" items, and check the paper's licensing and wording first.

### Gaps
- Exact item wording of the Chandler et al. (2011) scale was not retrieved (paywalled; DOI 10.1016/j.jbusvent.2009.10.006).
- I found no validated instrument that measures "bird-in-hand means inventory" as a pre-venture self-report for matching.

## 2. Holland RIASEC interests: free short instruments and use for team complementarity

### Takeaway
The O*NET Interest Profiler has official short versions: the 60-item Short Form and the 30-item Mini-IP, built for mobile. Both are free to use and translated/validated in several languages. The 30-item Mini-IP fits an onboarding flow. However, RIASEC predicts which activities a person enjoys, not team performance. Use it to infer preferred role (e.g., E = sales/leadership, C = admin/finance, R = craft/operations, A = creative/product, S = customer/community, I = analysis/R&D), not as a hard matching criterion.

### Cited Findings
- The O*NET Interest Profiler Short Form has 60 items (10 per RIASEC scale), derived from the 180-item long form. It was built to keep the hexagonal structure and to improve endorsement of the Enterprising scale. — [O*NET IPSF Psychometric report (2010)](https://www.onetcenter.org/reports/IPSF_Psychometric.html)
- The Mini-IP has 30 items (5 per scale), drawn from the Short-IP and designed for mobile devices. Internal consistency was acceptable, and 73% of participants received the same first-letter RIASEC code as on the Short-IP. Authors: Rounds, Ming, Cao, Song, Lewis (March 2016). — [O*NET Mini-IP report](https://www.onetcenter.org/reports/Mini-IP.html)
- Items use a 5-point scale from "Strongly Dislike" to "Strongly Like". Examples are "Build kitchen cabinets" (R) and "Develop a new medicine" (I). The 30-item set is available via O*NET Web Services. — [O*NET Web Services reference: ip_questions_30](https://services.onetcenter.org/reference/mnm/ip/ip_questions_30)
- A German adaptation (Roemer, Lewis & Rounds, 2023) showed adequate internal consistency and RIASEC structure. A Brazilian Portuguese adaptation found acceptable validity but only partial confirmation of the hexagon. — [DOAJ: Psychological Test Adaptation and Development 2023](https://doaj.org/article/7939fb544ac34660a645b4a102f42213); [Mackenzie PTP: Brazilian Portuguese adaptation](https://mackenzie.emnuvens.com.br/ptp/article/view/12022)
- O*NET tools are offered free of charge. Licensing is not confirmed as public domain/CC BY from a primary source, so check the O*NET terms. — [O*NET Mini-IP report](https://www.onetcenter.org/reports/Mini-IP.html)

### Inferences
- Use the Mini-IP (30 items, about 3–4 min) as an optional "Discover your work style" module, not in the mandatory first step. Offer German/Portuguese validated translations where available. Other languages need translation work, and their validity is unknown.
- For complementarity, the hexagon suggests pairing adjacent or opposite codes to cover functions (e.g., an A- or R-dominant craftsperson with an E/C-dominant partner for sales and admin). This is a design heuristic, not an evidence-based team-performance predictor.
- Item content (e.g., "develop a new medicine") is US-occupation flavored. Expat users from varied cultures may need localized wording.

### Gaps
- IPIP-based RIASEC scales (public-domain alternative): I found no primary source in this session. Verify at ipip.ori.org.
- I found no study showing that RIASEC complementarity in founding teams predicts venture outcomes.

## 3. Skill taxonomies: ESCO vs O*NET vs LinkedIn for multilingual, tech and non-tech users

### Takeaway
ESCO is the best fit for multilingual EU expat users. It has about 13,890 skills and about 3,039 occupations in 28 languages, including Ukrainian and Arabic, and it is free via API and download. But its granularity is too fine for self-report UX. Use a two-level approach: a small custom set of 30–60 "venture functions/skill clusters" for the questionnaire, mapped behind the scenes to ESCO skill URIs (and optionally O*NET) for search, translation and interoperability.

### Cited Findings
- ESCO v1.2 has approximately 3,039 occupations and 13,890 skills, published in 28 languages: the 24 official EU languages plus Norwegian, Icelandic, Ukrainian and Arabic. This comes from secondary metadata; ESCO itself confirms 28 languages. — [Dublin Core MSI: ESCO](https://msi.dublincore.org/standards/esco); [ESCO v1 page](https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en/about-esco/escopedia/escopedia/esco-v1)
- ESCO v1.2 was presented on 21 May 2024. Occupations and skills are available on the portal, as a download, and via API. — [ESCO v1.2 launch Q&A (PDF)](https://esco.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2024-05/V1.2%20Launch%20-%20ESCO%20SEC%20combined%20-%20final%20clean.pdf); [ESCO FAQ](https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en/about-esco/faq)
- ESCO v1.2.1 (December 2025) brought quality improvements to structure and relationships, with no content added or removed. — [ESCO v1 page](https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en/about-esco/escopedia/escopedia/esco-v1)

### Inferences
- **ESCO pros:** multilingual labels (critical for expats), coverage of trades, crafts, beauty and hospitality (non-tech venture types), EU-official, free API, and transversal skills (e.g., language, digital, entrepreneurial) usable as a separate layer.
- **ESCO cons:** about 14k skills is too many to browse. Labels are formal ("perform manicure" style). Startup-native skills (growth hacking, no-code, specific frameworks) lag behind.
- **O*NET:** US-centric and English-first, with rich ability/work-activity descriptors and free web services. Useful for the Interest Profiler and as a secondary mapping, less so for multilingual UX.
- **LinkedIn skills:** proprietary, with no free open API for the taxonomy. Avoid it as the backbone, though users can import.
- **Recommended granularity:**
  - Level 1: about 8 venture functions. These are product/craft delivery, sales and customer acquisition, marketing/brand/content, operations/logistics/supply, finance/accounting/admin, legal/compliance/local bureaucracy, tech/digital, and people/community/teaching.
  - Level 2: about 5–10 clusters per function (about 40–60 total), which users pick.
  - Level 3: optional free-text or autocomplete against ESCO skills in the user's language for specifics (e.g., "barista", "React", "customs declarations"). Store ESCO URIs.
- For each selected cluster, ask a proficiency level with behavioral anchors (see section 5). Tag it CAN / COULD (learnable within about 3 months) / WANT (would enjoy doing).

### Gaps
- I did not verify the exact ESCO API rate limits or license text (the EU reuse policy is generally permissive). Check the ESCO portal "Download / API" pages.
- I found no comparative study on taxonomy usability for self-report by immigrants.

## 4. Business-founding role models for any venture type (balanced skills, functional roles, team composition)

### Takeaway
Lazear's jack-of-all-trades theory, that entrepreneurs need balanced skills across many functions, is broadly supported for entry into entrepreneurship and progress in early ventures. The evidence is mostly cross-sectional. For matching, the goal is to cover functions at the team level: either one balanced generalist plus specialists, or specialists who jointly cover all core functions. Functional heterogeneity helps when it widens scope, but too much disparity between members can hurt.

### Cited Findings
- Variety in career history predicts entry into entrepreneurship, partly because variety makes a person more likely to have certain specific skills. — [Springer: J Technol Transf (s10961-014-9347-x)](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10961-014-9347-x); [SAGE ETAP 10.1111/etap.12220](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1111/etap.12220)
- Stuetzer, Obschonka & Schmitt-Rodermund (2013): balanced skills matter for progress in high-potential nascent ventures. Prior managerial or entrepreneurial experience and early entrepreneurial interest were linked to more balanced skills. — [QUT eprints: Balanced skills among nascent entrepreneurs](https://eprints.qut.edu.au/218661); [QUT eprints 50669](https://eprints.qut.edu.au/50669)
- Goethner & Cantner (2012): nascent entrepreneurs with broad enough skill sets carry out more gestation activities. — [MPRA 37524](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/37524/1/MPRA_paper_37524.pdf)
- Critique (Lechmann & Schnabel 2014): most tests are cross-sectional, and the core assumption that the self-employed perform many tasks had not been directly tested. — [IZA conference paper, Schnabel](https://conference.iza.org/EntreRes2012/schnabel_c788.pdf)
- Jin et al. (2017, ETP) meta-analysis (55 samples, N=8,892): team aggregated characteristics, heterogeneity and team size each have significant, distinct effects on new venture performance. I did not see the direction for functional heterogeneity specifically. — [IDEAS/RePEc: Jin et al. 2017](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/entthe/v41y2017i5p743-771.html); [Lazaridis Institute summary](https://lazaridisinstitute.wlu.ca/research/research-activities/curated-articles/articles/2017/i-september/entrepreneurial-team-composition.html)
- Cantner, Goethner & Stuetzer (2010), 337 start-up teams: knowledge **scope** (breadth) helps entrepreneurial performance, while knowledge **disparity** (members too far apart) relates negatively to performance. — [IDEAS/RePEc Jena WP 2010-029](https://ideas.repec.org/p/jrp/jrpwrp/2010-029.html)

### Inferences
- Matching objective: maximize team coverage of the venture-type-specific required functions, while keeping a "shared ground" overlap. Examples are a common language, shared domain interest and similar ambition, which help avoid excessive disparity.
- Required functions differ by venture type. Define templates:
  - **Café:** craft/product (barista, chef), operations, sales/local marketing, finance/admin, legal/permits.
  - **Import/export:** sourcing network, logistics/customs, sales, finance, languages.
  - **Tech startup:** build, sell, design. This is the "hacker/hustler/hipster" archetype (see Gaps).
- A "jack-of-all-trades score" (number of functions with at least basic proficiency) can be computed from the questionnaire and used to suggest a coordinator/CEO-type role.

### Gaps
- Hacker/hustler/hipster (or "hacker/hustler/designer") is a widely used startup-culture heuristic. I found no academic source in this session; treat it as practitioner folklore.
- I found no validated functional-role taxonomy for micro/small businesses (cafés, beauty, crafts). The function list above is a synthesis.

## 5. Self-assessment accuracy and mitigation

### Takeaway
People's self-ratings of ability correlate only modestly with actual performance (r ≈ .3 across meta-analyses). The classic Dunning–Kruger pattern is partly a statistical artifact (regression to the mean plus the better-than-average effect). Either way, raw "rate yourself 1–5" scores are noisy. Mitigate with behavioral anchors, concrete or numeric questions, and evidence and verification layers.

### Cited Findings
- Meta-analysis: pooled correlation between estimated and actual task performance is r = .32 (CI .29–.35). — [JEMS meta-analysis](https://scapps.org/jems/index.php/1/article/view/1095)
- Self-estimated versus measured intelligence: r ≈ .33 across 41 studies. Numerical/specific self-estimates were more accurate than general ones (Freund & Kasten). — [PMC8883889](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8883889/)
- The Dunning–Kruger effect in creativity appeared under classical quartile analysis but not consistently under more rigorous methods, consistent with the artifact critique. — [Lebuda et al. 2024, Sci Rep (PMC11126607)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11126607/)
- A rational Bayesian account explains inaccurate self-assessment through prior beliefs and noisy judgment of one's own correctness, with weak support for a specific metacognitive deficit. — [Jansen, Rafferty & Griffiths (Princeton)](https://cocosci.princeton.edu/papers/DK_NHB_Final.pdf); [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/5fh7m0zt)

### Inferences
- Replace adjective scales ("beginner–expert") with **behaviorally anchored levels** per skill, CEFR-style "I can..." statements. For example, bookkeeping:
  - L1: "I've done my own personal budget."
  - L2: "I kept books for a small business with help."
  - L3: "I independently did a small business's books and tax filings for 1+ years."
  - L4: "I've managed finance for a team or company, or trained others."
- Ask **quantified experience facts** instead of ratings. Examples are years doing it, whether done for pay, largest project or customer count, and the last time done. The research shows specific, numeric self-estimates are more accurate.
- **Evidence layer:** optional portfolio links, photos of work (crafts, beauty, food), certificates or diplomas (and whether recognized in the host country), and references.
- **Social verification:** peer endorsements from people who worked with the user. Weight endorsements from completed in-app collaborations higher than from friends.
- **Trial tasks / micro-collaborations:** a small paid or unpaid test project before committing. This is consistent with effectuation's experimentation.
- Show matched partners the evidence level ("self-reported" / "portfolio" / "endorsed by 2") rather than hiding uncertainty.
- Expat-specific: credentials from abroad are often under-recognized, so self-reports may under-state skills. Ask both "level of skill" and "is this formally recognized here?"

### Gaps
- Zell & Krizan (2014) meta-analysis was not retrieved directly. Its reported overall self-performance correlation is commonly cited as about .29, but that was not verified here.
- I found no direct studies on self-report accuracy among migrants or cross-cultural response styles (e.g., modesty bias). This is likely relevant but unverified.

## 6. Ikigai framing; commitment and ambition alignment

### Takeaway
The popular four-circle "ikigai" Venn diagram (love / good at / world needs / paid for) is not Japanese in origin. It is Andrés Zuzunaga's "purpose" diagram (2011–12), relabeled by Marc Winn in 2014, and it has no validation as an instrument. It works as a friendly UX metaphor for CAN / WANT / (market need) but should not be presented as science. Commitment and ambition mismatch is a well-known founder-team risk. Wasserman's data show team instability, but direct evidence on "lifestyle vs scale" mismatch was not found. Still, asking explicit commitment and ambition questions is low-cost and high-value.

### Cited Findings
- The Venn diagram was created by Spanish astrologer Andrés Zuzunaga as "propósito" (purpose). It was published in Borja Vilaseca's 2012 book and posted on Facebook in June 2012. Marc Winn later merged it with the ikigai label ("my role was merely to merge the concept of Ikigai with this already defined concept of purpose"). Mieko Kamiya's original Japanese ikigai work contains no such diagram. Sources are blogs/secondary, not primary documents. — [Ikigai Tribe: How the Zuzunaga diagram became ikigai](https://ikigaitribe.com/?p=3016); [Ikigai Tribe: Ikigai misunderstood](https://ikigaitribe.com/?p=1348); [business-on.de](https://www.business-on.de/ikigai.html)
- Wasserman's *The Founder's Dilemmas* (about 10,000 founders): friendship-based founding teams were the least stable, with each friendship raising founder turnover by 28.6%. Former coworkers were the most stable teams. — [WEF: Why friends shouldn't go into business together (Adam Grant)](https://www.weforum.org/stories/business/why-friends-shouldnt-go-into-business-together/)
- Flashpoints are money, credit, blame, leadership and strategy. Equity advice is to wait until commitment and contributions are known, because early equal splits often mismatch later roles. — [Next Big Idea Club (Grant on Wasserman)](https://nextbigideaclub.com/magazine/adam-grant-best-friend-might-not-best-business-partner/5053/); [Tech.co summary](https://tech.co/founders-dilemmas-noam-wasserman-2013-10)
- The "rich vs king" trade-off: control-minded founders tend to go it alone, while wealth-minded founders bring in co-founders and investors. — [Shortform overview](https://www.shortform.com/blog/?p=145189)
- The widely cited "65% of high-potential startups fail due to co-founder conflict" is attributed to Wasserman in news reports, but I could not verify it against the book. — [Forbes Argentina](https://www.forbesargentina.com/negocios/el-lado-oscuro-equity-cuando-cofounders-logran-ponerse-acuerdo-n67612/amp)

### Inferences
- Use ikigai only as a visual metaphor for the profile summary (CAN ∩ WANT ∩ market need). Add a small caveat or avoid calling it "Japanese wisdom" in the UI.
- Mandatory alignment questions (hard filters or strong weights in matching):
  - **Commitment:** hours/week now; hours/week in 6 months; side project vs part-time vs full-time; start date.
  - **Ambition:** a lifestyle/income-replacement business, a solid local SME (1–10 staff), a regional/multi-location business, or a high-growth/investor-funded venture.
  - **Control vs wealth:** "I'd rather own 100% of a small business / a small share of a big one."
  - **Exit horizon:** build to keep, sell, or undecided.
  - **Money in vs money needed:** affordable loss and income need (see section 1).
  - **Risk tolerance:** banded scale.
  - **Decision style and values:** e.g., formal vs informal, speed vs thoroughness.
- Given Wasserman's friends/coworkers finding, offer "trial collaboration" before formal partnership, and prompt early founder agreements (roles, equity, vesting, exit), especially since many expat teams form via diaspora/friend networks.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study specifically on "lifestyle vs growth ambition mismatch → team breakup". This is an inference from Wasserman's conflict sources (strategy, money) and from practitioner consensus.
- I found no validated ikigai instrument.

## 7. Expat-specific resources: permits, language, local knowledge, diaspora networks

### Takeaway
Legal status is a hard constraint and must be asked about in a structured, privacy-sensitive way. In Germany, self-employment requires a specific title or permission (§21 AufenthG has five routes). Blue Card holders and German graduates have easier routes, but the business must relate to their qualifications. Language should be self-assessed using the official CEFR self-assessment grid (5 skills × 6 levels, free, available in many languages). Diaspora networks are an asset for sourcing, import/export and first customers, but evidence is mixed: family/local ties can also constrain.

### Cited Findings
- §21 AufenthG has five routes to self-employment:
  - §21(1): general business. Requires economic interest or regional need, a positive economic effect, and secured financing. The permit is usually for up to 3 years.
  - §21(5): liberal-profession freelancers, with easier requirements.
  - §21(2a): German university graduates, researchers/scientists and EU Blue Card holders can get a permit without the stricter tests, if the activity relates to their studies or qualifications.
  - A settlement permit is possible after 3 years if the business succeeds and income is secured.
  — [MTH Partner: §21 AufenthG](https://www.mth-partner.de/en/immigration-law/foreigners-law-residence-permit-for-self-employment-and-freelance-activity-according-to-21-of-the-residence-act-aufenthg/); [MTH Partner overview (2026)](https://www.mth-partner.de/en/?p=13359); [Uni Münster International Founders Network handbook](https://www.uni-muenster.de/imperia/md/content/wwu/auslandsamt/international-founders-network_handbook_entrepreneurship_module_visa-and-residence-permit.pdf)
- EU Single Permit Directive recast (2024/1233): transposition deadline 21 May 2026, applying from 22 May 2026. It strengthens the right to change employer. In July 2026 the Commission sent formal notices to 17 Member States, including DE, FR, PT and NL, for incomplete transposition. — [EP Legislative Observatory](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/document-summary?id=1784560); [ieu-monitoring (16 Jul 2026)](https://ieu-monitoring.com/editorial/eu-commission-takes-action-to-ensure-complete-and-timely-transposition-of-directives/1245795/); [MLGTS legal alert](https://www.mlgts.pt/en/knowledge/legal-alerts/Legal-Alert-Single-Authorisation-for-third-country-workers-notes-on-Directive-EU-20241233/27019/)
- CEFR self-assessment grid (Council of Europe/Europass) covers listening, reading, spoken interaction, spoken production and writing × A1–C2, with "I can" descriptors. Levels A = basic, B = independent, C = proficient. The detailed grid is recommended over the short global scale for self-assessment. — [Europass CEFR self-assessment grid (PDF)](https://europass.europa.eu/system/files/2020-05/CEFR%20self-assessment%20grid%20EN.pdf); [Council of Europe copy](https://rm.coe.int/16802fc1cc)
- Diaspora networks can provide opportunity recognition, resource access and motivation. — [JEMI: Network dynamics of descending diaspora entrepreneurship](https://jemi.edu.pl/vol-10-issue-4-2014/network-dynamics-of-descending-diaspora-entrepreneurship-multiple-case-studies-with-japanese-entrepreneurs-in-emerging-economies)
- Local family ties may be counterproductive for transnational entrepreneurship. — [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/978-3-319-72047-0_7)
- Some diaspora entrepreneurs mostly do local business despite transnational backgrounds. — [Elo & Jokela, UTU](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/3348542?lang=en_US)
- The term "diaspora entrepreneur" is often poorly defined. — [Emerald IJEBR 2022](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/IJEBR-08-2021-0658/full/pdf)

### Inferences
- **Legal status question design:**
  - Do not ask for document numbers.
  - Ask the permit category in a banded, country-specific list: EU/EEA citizen; permanent/settlement; Blue Card; employment permit tied to employer; student; job-seeker; self-employment permit; freelance permit; asylum/temporary protection (e.g., Ukrainians under the Temporary Protection Directive); family reunification; other/unsure.
  - Then derive "can legally be self-employed / co-owner / employee now?" as yes / needs permission / unsure.
  - Let users answer "unsure" and link to official resources.
  - Treat this as sensitive (GDPR special category-adjacent). Make it optional, show it only as a derived "can be formal co-founder now: yes / with approval" flag, and get explicit consent.
- Note the distinction between owning shares or being a passive investor and actively working in the business. Permit rules differ, so the matching should allow roles like "silent partner / investor / advisor" for those who cannot work self-employed yet.
- **Language:** embed a compact CEFR grid (5 skills × 6 levels, with descriptor text shown on tap) for the host-country language, English, and native/other languages. Store per language. Many matches hinge on "one partner is C1 in the local language".
- **Local market knowledge:**
  - Years in the city/country.
  - Sectors worked in locally.
  - Experience with local bureaucracy (registered a business, did a tax return, rented commercial premises, hired staff).
  - These should be checkbox "I have done X here" items, which are behaviorally anchored.
- **Cross-border networks:** countries where the user has active business contacts, contact types (suppliers, distributors, customers, officials, community associations), and the size of the diaspora community they can reach.
- **Location:** city, willingness to relocate within the country, remote OK.

### Gaps
- I did not verify other EU countries' self-employment permit rules (FR talent passport, NL DAFT for US/JP citizens, PT D2 visa, ES digital nomad visa). Each needs a country-specific lookup.
- I could not verify the exact Single Permit Directive text on unemployment periods or change of employer.
- Whether Germany completed transposition after the July 2026 notice is unknown.
- I found no quantitative evidence on diaspora advantage effect sizes.

## 8. UX: questionnaire length, progressive profiling, completion rates

### Takeaway
Completion drops with each added question, most sharply up to about 15 questions and beyond about 7–8 minutes. So the first-run questionnaire should be short (about 10–15 screens, under 5–7 min), enough for a first match. Everything else (RIASEC module, detailed skills, evidence, endorsements) should come through progressive profiling with completeness meters and match-quality incentives.

### Cited Findings
- SurveyMonkey analysis of about 100,000 surveys (1–50 questions): each added question raises drop-off most sharply up to 15 questions. Beyond 35 questions, respondents appear largely indifferent to length. Abandonment rises for surveys over 7–8 minutes, with completion dropping 5–20%. This is vendor data, not peer-reviewed. — [SurveyMonkey: Survey questions and completion rates](https://www.surveymonkey.com/curiosity/survey_questions_and_completion_rates/)
- Vendor data (SurveyRock, about 400k responses): completion 98% at 11–15 questions versus 77% at 16–25. Most abandonment happens before the halfway point. — [SurveyRock benchmarks](https://www.surveyrock.com/blog/survey-completion-rate-benchmarks)
- Progressive disclosure is credited to Carroll & Rosson (IBM) and popularized by Nielsen (1995). Completeness meters (e.g., LinkedIn profile strength "All-Star") ease time-commitment worries and use a sense of incompleteness to drive completion. These are secondary sources. — [Goodreads-hosted article: Web form design patterns](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/10225589-web-form-design-best-practices-5-useful-ui-patterns); [dsebastien concepts: Progressive Disclosure](https://concepts.dsebastien.net/concept/progressive-disclosure/)
- The US Web Design System "complex form" pattern recommends showing progress and letting users save and continue. — [digital.gov USWDS pattern](https://designsystem.digital.gov/patterns/complete-a-complex-form/progress-easily/)
- Claims that multi-step forms beat single-page forms by 20–40% are unsourced. Treat them as anecdotal. — [OrbitForms blog](https://orbitforms.ai/blog/what-is-progressive-disclosure-in-forms)

### Inferences
- Suggested staging:
  - **Stage 0, onboarding (about 3–5 min, ≤15 items):**
    - Venture interest (type tags plus own idea or join others).
    - Role intent (lead / co-founder / partner / investor / advisor).
    - Top 3 functions CAN, with one anchored level each.
    - Top 3 WANT.
    - Commitment hours and timing.
    - Ambition band.
    - Money band (affordable loss).
    - City.
    - Languages (CEFR quick level for 2–3 languages).
    - Legal can-be-self-employed flag (optional).
  - **Stage 1, after the first match preview:** COULD (learnable) skills, network assets ("I can introduce..."), local-knowledge checklist, and evidence/portfolio.
  - **Stage 2, optional:** Mini-IP RIASEC (30 items, framed as a "work style" result the user receives), values/working style, and full CEFR grid.
  - **Stage 3, ongoing:** endorsements, trial-collaboration outcomes, and periodic re-confirmation of availability (commitment changes fast).
- Give users value back at each step (e.g., a RIASEC profile, "your team gaps", match count rising). Show "profile strength" tied to match quality.
- Use chips or multi-select over free text, autocomplete against localized ESCO labels, and mobile-first design. Let the UI language differ from the answers language.

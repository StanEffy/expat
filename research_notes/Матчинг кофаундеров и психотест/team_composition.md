# Team Composition Science: Who Works Best With Whom (for co-founder / small-team matching)

Scope note: Notes cover team-level personality, fit (similarity vs complementarity), role models (Belbin), collective intelligence, founding-team research, immigrant entrepreneurship, and team breakup predictors. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** come from background knowledge and were not confirmed against a source in this session. They are listed under Gaps and should be checked before anyone cites them.

## 1. Team-level personality composition (Bell 2007, bad apples, Honesty-Humility)

### Takeaway
In real (field) teams, the strongest personality signals are the team's **minimum agreeableness** (the least agreeable member) and the team's **mean conscientiousness**. Openness, collectivism and preference for teamwork also matter. In lab teams, personality effects almost vanish. One low-agreeableness, low-effort or emotionally negative member can drag a small team down, and good members cannot fully offset that. Honesty-Humility (HEXACO) is one of the best trait predictors of cooperative or non-exploitative behaviour, but the individual-level effects are small.

### Cited Findings
- Bell (2007), *J. Applied Psychology* 92(3), 595–615, meta-analysis of deep-level composition. Effects are **moderated by setting (lab vs field) and by how the composition variable is operationalized** (mean, minimum, maximum, variance). — [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17484544/)
- Bell (2007) field studies: **team minimum agreeableness, team mean conscientiousness, openness, collectivism and preference for teamwork** were strong predictors of team performance. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17484544/)
- Bell (2007) lab studies: personality–performance effects were negligible. **Minimum and maximum general mental ability** predicted performance there. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17484544/)
- Meta-analytic estimates for team agreeableness and performance range from about **ρ = .34 (Bell 2007) to ρ = .51 (Peeters et al. 2006)**. This comes from a secondary vendor source (Alva Labs), not checked against the primary tables. — [Alva Labs](https://help.alvalabs.io/en/articles/3315430-team-personality-and-performance)
- Felps, Mitchell & Byington (2006), *Research in Organizational Behavior* 27, 175–222, is a review plus theoretical model, **not a meta-analysis**.
  - It defines a "bad apple" as someone who persistently does at least one of three things: **withholds effort**, **expresses negative affect**, or **violates interpersonal norms**.
  - The proposed mechanism runs in three steps. The behaviour leads teammates to feel inequity, negative mood and reduced trust. They respond defensively (outbursts, withdrawal). That reduces cooperation and creativity.
  - [Paper PDF](https://homepages.se.edu/cvonbergen/files/2013/01/HOW-WHEN-AND-WHY-BAD-APPLES-SPOIL-THE-BARREL.pdf); [Erasmus record](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/19964)
- UW press coverage of the work: it reviewed about two dozen studies of small teams. One study of about 50 manufacturing teams found that teams with a **disagreeable or irresponsible member had more conflict, worse communication and less cooperation, and performed poorly**. The press release gives **no percentage effect size**. — [UW News](https://www.washington.edu/news/?p=14623)
- Popular summaries say "one bad apple can spoil the barrel but one or two good workers can't unspoil it". This is a press-level framing of the theory. — [Mediate.com](https://mediate.com/recent-study-shows-bad-workplace-apples-do-indeed-spoil-the-barrel/)
- Thielmann, Spadaro & Balliet (2020), *Psychological Bulletin* 146(1), 30–90:
  - **770 studies and 3,523 effects** of 8 broad and 43 narrow traits on prosocial behaviour in 6 economic games.
  - Trait effects are small overall: **−.18 ≤ ρ ≤ .26**.
  - **Honesty-Humility** was among the three strongest predictors, alongside Social Value Orientation and Guilt Proneness. Its effect was **strongest in situations that allow exploitation**.
  - [Psychology Today summary](https://www.psychologytoday.com/intl/blog/individual-differences/202001/where-are-the-prosocial-people); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7657889)
- Caveat: Columbus, Thielmann & Balliet (2019) found **only inconsistent evidence** for the Honesty-Humility–prosocial link when they tested situational moderators. — [St Andrews portal](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/situational-affordances-for-prosocial-behaviour-on-the-interactio/)

### Inferences
- For matching, the team composition rule should be **"screen out the low tail"**, not "maximize the average". In small teams of 2–4, the lowest agreeableness, conscientiousness or integrity score is what matters, so use a minimum or threshold rule rather than a sum.
- Conscientiousness behaves additively (the mean matters), so everyone's level counts.
- The three bad-apple dimensions map well onto measurable pre-match signals:
  - effort and reliability (conscientiousness, plus a behavioural track record such as finishing app tasks and keeping commitments);
  - emotional negativity (neuroticism);
  - norm violation (low agreeableness and low Honesty-Humility).
- Honesty-Humility is a sensible "safety" filter, because co-founding means joint money and equity, which is a high-exploitation situation. Expect only modest predictive power, so pair it with behavioural signals such as reputation and references.
- Lab-based effects do not transfer, so validate any matching algorithm on real, long-running teams.

### Gaps
- The exact Bell (2007) effect sizes were not verified in the primary tables. **[UNVERIFIED, from memory]** field ρ values of roughly .33–.40 for min agreeableness, mean conscientiousness, collectivism and preference for teamwork; roughly .20 for mean openness; variance effects mostly small or negative.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Prewett et al. (2009) and Peeters et al. (2006) meta-analyses: the specifics were not retrieved.
- No source found on team-level aggregation of Honesty-Humility (mean vs minimum) and team or co-founder outcomes. The evidence is individual-level, from economic games.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** A frequently cited Felps lab experiment with a planted confederate ("bad apple" groups performing 30–40% worse) could not be confirmed. The UW release gives no such number.

## 2. Similarity vs complementarity (person-group fit, deep vs surface diversity)

### Takeaway
Fit with one's group predicts better attitudes and cohesion, with generalizable effects. Over time, **deep-level** similarity and difference (values, conscientiousness, how much the work matters to each person) become more important than **surface-level** demographic differences. Demographic differences mainly hurt early cohesion. A practical synthesis: **be similar on values, goals, commitment and work ethic; be complementary on skills, functions and networks**.

### Cited Findings
- Kristof-Brown, Zimmerman & Johnson (2005), *Personnel Psychology* 58, 281–342: meta-analysis of **172 studies and 836 effect sizes** across person-job, person-organization, person-group and person-supervisor fit. Nearly all credibility intervals excluded zero, so the effects generalize broadly. — [Iowa repository](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/CONSEQUENCES-OF-INDIVIDUALS-FIT-AT-WORK/9984380518202771)
- A 2024 latent-profile study of perceived person-group fit found that **higher person-group fit is not universally positive**. Different fit profiles predicted satisfaction, cohesion, performance, citizenship and withdrawal differently. — [SUFE abstract](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/368867)
- Harrison, Price & Bell (1998): the longer group members worked together, the **weaker the effects of surface-level diversity and the stronger the effects of deep-level diversity**. — [SUFE abstract](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/363599)
- Harrison, Price, Gavin & Florey (2002), *Academy of Management Journal* 45(5), 1029–1045, studied 144 student project teams:
  - Surface-level diversity hurt early cohesion.
  - Over time, actual and perceived deep-level diversity became more important for social cohesion and performance. Deep-level diversity here meant conscientiousness, task meaningfulness and outcome importance.
  - Time and collaboration are the medium through which deep-level information becomes visible.
  - [Paper (ResearchGate PDF)](https://researchgate.net/profile/David-Harrison-17/publication/234021571_Time_Teams_and_Task_Performance_Changing_Effects_of_Surface-_and_Deep-Level_Diversity_on_Group_Functioning/links/544827220cf2f14fb814208e/Time-Teams-and-Task-Performance-Changing-Effects-of-Surface-and-Deep-Level-Diversity-on-Group-Functioning.pdf); the detail on the deep-level measures comes from a secondary summary ([blog](https://archivedblog.jonobacon.com/2008/11/20/the-diversity-level/)).
- Wasserman's *Founder's Dilemmas* warns against founders "flocking together", meaning teams too similar in background. The source is a book summary. — [tech.co](https://tech.co/founders-dilemmas-noam-wasserman-2013-10)

### Inferences
- Match people on **deep-level goal alignment**:
  - ambition level, such as a lifestyle business vs a growth venture;
  - time commitment;
  - risk tolerance;
  - how important the venture's outcome is to each person;
  - work ethic.
  These are exactly the deep-level variables that grow in importance over time.
- Do not over-weight nationality or language similarity. Surface-level differences matter mostly early, and their effect fades with interaction. Help early cohesion instead with structured trial collaborations (small paid gigs, pilot projects) so that deep-level information surfaces quickly.
- Complementarity should apply to **skills, functions and local networks**, for example a person with local market and regulatory knowledge plus a person with a craft or service skill. It should not apply to values or effort.

### Gaps
- The Kristof-Brown (2005) person-group fit effect sizes could not be extracted; the PDF was an image scan. **[UNVERIFIED, from memory]**: person-group fit relates strongly to coworker satisfaction (ρ ≈ .4–.5) and group cohesion, moderately to job satisfaction, and only weakly to performance (ρ ≈ .2).
- No meta-analysis was found that directly compares supplementary (similarity) and complementary fit for co-founder outcomes.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** van Knippenberg & Schippers (2007) and Stahl et al. (2010) on cultural diversity in teams (process losses vs creativity gains) were not retrieved.

## 3. Belbin team roles: validity and alternatives

### Takeaway
Belbin's model is popular, but its psychometric support is weak to mixed. Its Self-Perception Inventory shows poor discriminant validity, and one confirmatory factor analysis found the model overparameterized. It should not be the backbone of a matching algorithm. Big Five or HEXACO traits, plus explicit skill and function complementarity, are better supported.

### Cited Findings
- Aritzeta, Swailes & Senior (2007), *Journal of Management Studies* 44(1), 96–118, reviewed 43 empirical studies. They found **adequate convergent validity** but **weak discriminant validity** among some Self-Perception Inventory scales, with strong associations between some roles. — [Huddersfield record](https://pure.hud.ac.uk/en/publications/belbins-team-role-model-development-validity-and-applications-for/); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jomstd/v44y2007i1p96-118.html)
- Fisher, Hunter & Macrosson (2001), using confirmatory factor analysis, found the Belbin model **overparameterized**, lacking both convergent and discriminant validity. — [Strathprints](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/1706/)
- Psychometric criticism dates back to Furnham, Steele & Pendleton (1993) and Broucek & Randell (1996). Belbin's reply was that the instruments were intended for consulting, not scholarly inquiry. — [en-academic summary](https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/2941526)
- A Belbin-affiliated source cites van Dierendonck & Groen (2011) as reporting good validity for a revised measure. It is **vendor-sourced and not verified**. — [Belbin review PDF](https://www.belbin.com/media/1158/belbin-uk-2014-a-comprehensive-review.pdf)

### Inferences
- Do not market role labels such as "Plant" or "Shaper" as scientific matching. Belbin-like language can serve as a soft UX vocabulary for self-reflection, but compute matches from validated trait measures and explicit skill inventories.
- A defensible alternative is a simple functional-role checklist:
  - who handles product or craft;
  - who handles sales and customers;
  - who handles money, admin and legal (critical for immigrants dealing with local bureaucracy);
  - who handles operations.
  Combine it with Big Five or HEXACO thresholds.

### Gaps
- **[UNVERIFIED]** The Mumford et al. (2008) Team Role Test (situational judgment test; task, social and boundary-spanning roles), which has better published validity, was not retrieved in this session but is a candidate alternative.
- No evidence was found that Belbin role balance predicts founding-team or small-business outcomes.

## 4. Collective intelligence (Woolley et al. 2010 and later work)

### Takeaway
There is meta-analytic support for a general group performance factor "c". The 2021 meta-analysis by Woolley's own group links it to collaboration process, social perceptiveness and the share of women. Independent critiques dispute how distinct c is from members' individual IQ, and most studies were underpowered. Treat c as **contested**, and do not use it as a matching criterion.

### Cited Findings
- Riedl, Kim, Gupta, Malone & Woolley (2021), *PNAS* 118(21) e2005737118:
  - meta-analytic methods on **22 studies, 5,279 individuals and 1,356 groups**;
  - strong support for a general c factor;
  - **collaboration process predicts CI more than individual member skill**;
  - CI is predicted by the **proportion of women, mediated by average social perceptiveness**;
  - CI predicts out-of-sample criterion tasks.
  - [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8166150); [CMU news](https://www.cmu.edu/tepper/news/stories/2021/may/collective-intelligence-research.html) (CMU reports 5,349 individuals, a minor discrepancy)
- Credé & Howardson (2017), *Journal of Applied Psychology*, re-analyzed the Woolley data and concluded it **did not support the basic premise of c**: the general factor was not strongly enough correlated with all tasks. Woolley, Kim & Malone replied, disputing that interpretation. — [SUFE abstract](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/370165); [Reply on SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3187373)
- Bates & Gupta (2016 preprint; 2017 *Intelligence*) ran three studies with 312 people. Contrary to the c hypothesis, **individual IQ accounted for group performance**. — [Preprint PDF](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/iq/2016-bates.pdf)
- Rowe, Hattie & Hester (2021) compared g and c across two meta-analyses:
  - moderate average inter-task correlation for c, **r = .26 (95% CI .10–.40)**;
  - about **80% of studies were underpowered**;
  - they advise against embracing c until it is independently replicated and shown to have incremental validity over g.
  - [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8019454/)

### Inferences
- The most robust, actionable part of this literature is that **process** (turn-taking, communication) and **social perceptiveness** matter. These can be supported by app design, such as structured kickoff meetings and communication norms, rather than used as a hard matching criterion.
- Avoid claims like "our algorithm maximizes your team's collective intelligence". The evidence is contested.

### Gaps
- No large pre-registered independent replication of c was found in this session.
- No evidence was found linking c to entrepreneurial or small-business outcomes.

## 5. Founding-team research (Wasserman, Ruef et al., diversity, conflict)

### Takeaway
Founders strongly self-select homophilous teams: same gender and ethnicity, often spouses or family, recruited through strong ties. Equal equity splits are common, especially when decided quickly, and are associated with worse funding outcomes, though this likely reflects selection. Relationship and process conflict are consistently harmful. Task conflict is roughly neutral on average and can help when it stays decoupled from relationship conflict. Entrepreneurial team composition has significant but modest meta-analytic effects on venture performance.

### Cited Findings
- Ruef, Aldrich & Carter (2003), *American Sociological Review* 68(2), 195–222, used a representative US sample of founding teams from the PSED (Panel Study of Entrepreneurial Dynamics).
  - They tested several mechanisms: homophily, functionality, status expectations, network constraint and ecological constraint.
  - Same-gender and same-ethnicity teams occur **more often than random matching would predict**.
  - Strong ties dominate recruitment, with a **high proportion of family members, especially spouses**.
  - [Duke Scholars](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1619740); [AcaWiki summary](https://acawiki.org/The_structure_of_founding_teams:_Homophily,_strong_ties,_and_isolation_among_U.S._entrepreneurs); [PDF](https://www.edegan.com/pdfs/ERBC/Sorenson,%205%2017%2011/Ruef_Aldrich_Carter_2003.pdf)
- Hellmann & Wasserman (2017), *Management Science*, "The First Deal", studied 1,476 founders in 511 ventures.
  - About **one-third of teams split equity equally**.
  - Bigger differences in idea generation, prior startup experience and capital contribution made equal splits less likely. Larger teams were also less likely to split equally.
  - Equal splits were associated with **lower pre-money valuations**, more so when the split was **decided quickly**, and with a lower likelihood of raising outside funding. The authors attribute this to **selection, not causation**.
  - The estimated cost to the stronger founder of accepting an equal split is about **10% of equity, or $450K in net present value**.
  - [NBER digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/aug11/w16922.html); [HBS Working Knowledge](https://hbswk.hbs.edu/item/the-first-deal-the-division-of-founder-equity-in-new-ventures); [Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2474)
- Wasserman's *Founder's Dilemmas* (2012) is based on data on almost 10,000 founders. It addresses whether to co-found with friends or relatives, and how such choices can splinter teams. — [book listing](https://openlibrary.telkomuniversity.ac.id/pustaka/105245/the-founder-s-dilemmas-anticipating-and-avoiding-the-pitfalls-that-can-sink-a-startup.html); [tech.co summary](https://tech.co/founders-dilemmas-noam-wasserman-2013-10)
- Jin, Madison, Kraiczy, Kellermanns, Crook & Xi (2017), *Entrepreneurship Theory and Practice* 41(5), 743–771, meta-analyzed **55 samples and 8,892 observations**. Team composition has **significant, unique effects** on new venture performance across three dimensions: aggregated characteristics, heterogeneity, and team size. Prior evidence on team size was equivocal. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/entthe/v41y2017i5p743-771.html); [Lazaridis summary](https://lazaridisinstitute.wlu.ca/research/research-activities/curated-articles/articles/2017/i-september/entrepreneurial-team-composition.html)
- de Wit, Greer & Jehn (2012), *Journal of Applied Psychology* 97(2), 360–390, pooled **116 studies and 8,880 groups**.
  - Relationship and process conflict have **stable negative** relationships with group outcomes.
  - Task conflict has **no strong negative** association with performance, unlike De Dreu & Weingart (2003).
  - Task conflict is **more positive** when it is weakly correlated with relationship conflict, in **top management teams**, and when performance is measured as financial performance or decision quality.
  - [Ovid](https://www.ovid.com/journals/japsy/fulltext/10.1037/a0024844~the-paradox-of-intragroup-conflict-a-meta-analysis); [Erasmus repository](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/37902)

### Inferences
- Left unaided, people form teams through homophily and strong ties. That is where a platform adds value: it can widen the pool while still checking deep-level alignment.
- The app should **nudge explicit, deliberate equity and role agreements**. Quick equal splits are a warning sign. Templates with vesting or cliffs and contribution-based splits, adapted for non-tech businesses (profit share, capital vs sweat equity), address a documented failure point.
- For conflict:
  - Matching cannot prevent task disagreement, and should not try. Healthy debate over the work is fine.
  - What predicts damage is relationship and process conflict, meaning who does what, when and how. Clear role and process agreements directly target process conflict.
  - Low agreeableness and low Honesty-Humility are the plausible trait drivers of task disagreement spilling over into relationship conflict.

### Gaps
- **[UNVERIFIED]** The often-quoted claim that "65% of high-potential startups fail due to co-founder conflict" (attributed to Wasserman) has no primary source located. Treat it as folklore unless sourced.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Wasserman's finding that friend or family co-founder teams are less stable (higher founder departure) was not confirmed in this session.
- The pooled de Wit (2012) correlations could not be retrieved. **[UNVERIFIED, from memory]**:

  | Meta-analysis | Task conflict–performance | Relationship conflict–performance | Process conflict–performance |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | de Wit et al. 2012 | about −.01 | about −.17 | about −.20 |
  | De Dreu & Weingart 2003 | about −.23 | about −.22 | not reported here |

- The Jin et al. (2017) direction and size per dimension (for example, whether experience heterogeneity helps) was not retrieved.
- Ruef et al. sample size not verified: 816 teams versus "830 entrepreneurs" cited elsewhere.

## 6. Immigrant / expat entrepreneurship specifics

### Takeaway
Immigrants found businesses at substantially higher rates than natives. Preliminary evidence shows that **mixed immigrant–native founding teams outperform** both all-native and all-immigrant teams. The mixed-embeddedness framework explains this: co-ethnic networks give initial resources, but upward mobility depends on links into host-country markets, institutions and regulation.

### Cited Findings
- Azoulay, Jones, Kim & Miranda (2022), *AER: Insights* 4(1), 71–88, using US administrative data:
  - Immigrants act more as "job creators" than "job takers".
  - They play outsized roles in high-growth entrepreneurship.
  - Press reports an **~80% higher firm-founding rate** for immigrants versus US-born people. The authors say immigrants found more firms "in pretty much every size bucket". The exact 80% figure was not verified in the paper text.
  - [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/aerins/v4y2022i1p71-88.html); [Kellogg Insight](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/reprint/immigrants-to-the-u-s-create-more-jobs-than-they-take); [IPR WP](https://www.ipr.northwestern.edu/our-work/working-papers/2020/wp-20-45.html)
- Jin, Kermani & McQuade (NBER conference slides, 2024, **preliminary**):
  - Among startups with at least one immigrant founder, **42% are immigrant–native teams**.
  - Native–immigrant startups outperform native-only and immigrant-only firms: **20–50% larger employment, 20–100% more funding, 10–50% more successful exits**.
  - This is a high-growth / VC-oriented sample.
  - [NBER slides](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f211531/f211531.slides.pdf)
- Kloosterman, van der Leun & Rath (2001), studying Islamic butchers in the Netherlands, introduced **mixed embeddedness**.
  - Immigrant entrepreneurs depend on co-ethnic networks, including for informal activity.
  - Upward mobility requires **insertion into broader markets and host-country institutions**.
  - [IJURR](https://www.ijurr.org/article/mixed-embeddedness-informal-economic-activities-and-immigrant-businesses-in-the-netherlands/)
- Kloosterman & Rath (2018) describe mixed embeddedness as a versatile framework linking migrants' resources to the opportunity structure. — [janrath.com](https://www.janrath.com/news/journal-article-mixed-embeddedness-revisited/)
- Critiques of mixed embeddedness:
  - Peters (2002, Western Australia): it does not explain inter-ethnic variation, lacks a historical perspective and agency, and focuses on the lower end of the market. — [Emerald](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/13552550210423705/full/html)
  - A Southampton review flags missing attention to regulation, racist exclusion, gendered migration and market ghettoisation. — [Southampton](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/403335)
- Price & Chacko (2009), studying Ethiopian and Bolivian entrepreneurs in Washington DC: business entry was driven more by **blocked mobility and labour-market segmentation** than by ethnic-enclave formation. — [GWU PDF](https://gum.columbian.gwu.edu/sites/g/files/zaxdzs5166/files/downloads/Publications/Price%20and%20Chacko%202009.pdf)

### Inferences
- This is the strongest product-specific implication. Matching should deliberately support **bridging pairs**:
  - an expat or immigrant with a skill, capital or a home-country network;
  - plus a local or long-settled resident with host-market knowledge, language, regulatory know-how and customer access.
  Mixed teams show the best (preliminary) performance, and mixed embeddedness explains why.
- Co-ethnic teams are natural (homophily, trust, shared language) and give fast start-up resources such as informal finance, labour and first customers. The risk is lock-in to saturated co-ethnic niches. The app could value "host-market access" as a complementary skill dimension.
- Many immigrant ventures are necessity-driven (blocked mobility). Goal alignment (lifestyle or income stability vs growth) is especially important to check.

### Gaps
- Kerr & Kerr (2016, 2020) Survey of Business Owners results on immigrant-owned firm performance and co-ownership patterns were not retrieved.
- No rigorous evidence was found on **mixed vs co-ethnic teams in non-tech small businesses** (cafes, trades). The Jin–Kermani–McQuade evidence covers high-growth startups and is preliminary.
- No European data was retrieved (for example OECD or GEM immigrant entrepreneurship rates). Rates vary greatly by host country.

## 7. Predictors of partnership/team breakup and success in small businesses

### Takeaway
Team-member exit is common: up to about 40% of entrepreneurial teams lose a member. The predictors of who joins differ from the predictors of who leaves. **Heterogeneity in prior entrepreneurial experience predicts exits**, while **family firms see fewer exits**. Combined with the conflict literature, relationship and process conflict and unclear contribution and equity arrangements are the main documented breakup risks.

### Cited Findings
- Gregori & Parastuty (2021), *Review of Managerial Science*, systematic review:
  - The team-exit literature is fragmented. They organize it into antecedents, routes, consequences and context.
  - Exits have serious consequences for the leaver, the remaining team and venture performance.
  - Cited evidence suggests **up to 40% of entrepreneurial teams experience at least one member exit**.
  - [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11846-020-00377-1)
- Ucbasaran, Lockett, Wright & Westhead (2003), *Entrepreneurship Theory and Practice*, tracked 90 owner-managed (non-VC-focused) ventures from 1990 to 2000.
  - Predictors of **entry and exit differ**.
  - **Heterogeneity in prior entrepreneurial experience was positively associated with member exit.**
  - **Family firms were negatively associated with exit.**
  - [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/entthe/v28y2003i2p107-128.html)
- Exit routes (selling to an outside buyer vs to a teammate) depend on team characteristics that indicate cohesion. — [MetaTOC / ISBJ](https://metatoc.com/papers/95730-should-i-sell-my-shares-to-an-external-buyer-the-role-of-the-entrepreneurial-team-in-entrepreneurial-exit)
- See Section 5: equal equity splits decided quickly signal unexamined contribution differences ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/digest/aug11/w16922.html)). Relationship and process conflict reliably harm outcomes ([de Wit et al. 2012](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/37902)).

### Inferences
- Family and strong-tie teams appear more stable in membership (Ucbasaran), even though Wasserman-style VC evidence suggests they may be weaker on performance and conflict handling. The trade-off is **stability vs capability**.
- Differences in entrepreneurial experience predict exits. Matching should surface mismatched expectations, for example a serial founder paired with a first-timer. It should also prompt explicit agreements on decision rights, time commitment and exit or buy-out terms, such as a shareholder or partnership agreement with buy-sell clauses.
- An evidence-based **"pre-nup" module** is a strong feature to support with research. It would cover roles, equity or profit split rationale, vesting, conflict process and exit terms.

### Gaps
- No PSED-specific study of team dissolution in non-VC small businesses was retrieved in this session.
- No meta-analysis was found specifically on small-business partnership dissolution (for example, law or medical partnerships, or spousal businesses).
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Other candidate sources, not retrieved:
  - Breugst, Patzelt & Rathgeber (2015, *Journal of Business Venturing*), on how justice perceptions about equity splits affect team commitment;
  - Lazar et al. (2020, *Academy of Management Annals*), on entrepreneurial team formation;
  - Kauffman Foundation reports on partnerships and survival.
